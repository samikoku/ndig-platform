The $21 Billion Question Nobody Could Answer

NDIG Weekly · Issue #1 · Thursday 8 October 2026

Every year, Nigerians abroad send home more than twenty billion dollars. The exact figure changes with the source. The Central Bank says one number. The World Bank says another. The International Monetary Fund has its own count. What does not change is this: nobody can tell you, with certainty, where all that money goes.

Not the regulators. Not the banks. Not the receiving families. And certainly not the senders.

I have spent years watching this flow. I have sat with diaspora Nigerians in London, in Houston, in Johannesburg, who send money home every month and still cannot answer the simplest questions. Did the money arrive? Did it arrive whole? Did it go where it was supposed to go? Was the person who received it the person who was supposed to receive it?

These are not trick questions. They are the most basic questions in finance. And for the Nigerian diaspora, they have no reliable answer.

This is not a story about corruption. It is not a story about incompetence. It is a story about infrastructure. The pipes exist. The money moves. What is missing is the layer that answers those questions — a verification layer, a trust layer, a way to know.

That layer is what NDIG was built to provide.

NDIG does not move your money. It does not hold your money. It does not advise you where to put your money. What it does is verify. It verifies the people you deal with. It verifies the institutions that claim to serve you. It verifies the claims that are made to you, in your name, with your funds.

The twenty-one billion dollars is not the problem. The problem is that twenty-one billion dollars moves every year through a system where trust is assumed, not proven. NDIG exists to change that assumption into a standard.

This is the first issue of the NDIG Weekly. Every fortnight, we will take one question the diaspora cannot answer and answer it. Not with opinion. Not with promotion. With verification.

Until then, ask yourself the only question that matters: when you send money home, do you know, or do you trust?
