Questions

Five things people ask first

Is NDIG a government body?

No. NDIG is a private-sector, independent verification institution. It is regulator-vetted — its processes are reviewed against regulatory standards — but it is not a government agency, receives no government money, uses no government data, and carries no government endorsement.

Does NDIG hold or move my money?

Never. NDIG has no account in which members' funds sit, at any point. When you act on a verified opportunity, you transact directly with the official institution — its own portal, office, or licensed channel.

Does NDIG give investment advice?

No. NDIG verifies and informs. Whether and how you invest is your decision, made on your own judgment or with your own licensed adviser. Anything NDIG cannot verify is labelled as unverified — never presented as an opportunity.

What does membership cost?

The NDIG Weekly is free during the launch phase, which begins 1 October 2026. Paid membership tiers will be announced later; no card is required to join now, and you will never be charged without explicit notice and consent.

How are opportunities verified?

Each opportunity is checked against public regulatory records — corporate registrations, operating licences, and official registers relevant to the sector. Verification is against what is publicly provable, not what is privately promised. The result of that check is published with the listing.
